Holographic Distance — Help

What are we proving?

The Holographic Distance Theorem gives an exact formula for the distance between two token configurations on a tree:

d(x, y) = Σ_e |h_e(x) − h_e(y)|

Here x and y are two placements of k tokens on the nodes of a tree T, and d(x, y) is the minimum number of single-edge token moves needed to rearrange x into y.

The key quantity is the height h_e(x): for each edge e, cut the tree at e — the subtree on the far side from the root is T_e, and h_e(x) counts how many of x's tokens lie inside T_e.

The insight is that the height vector h(x) = (h_{e₁}(x), h_{e₂}(x), …) is a hologram of the configuration: it encodes all distances without any graph search, because it captures exactly how many tokens must cross each edge in any optimal path.

Proof structure (6 steps)

The proof is a sandwich argument — we show both a lower and upper bound equal to Σ|δ_e|, forcing equality with d(x, y).

Step What it does
1 · Setup Define the two configurations x, y and the tree.
2 · Heights Compute h_e(x) and h_e(y) for every edge; form δ_e = h_e(x) − h_e(y).
3 · Lemma One token move along edge f changes exactly one h_e (namely h_f) by ±1. This uses the fact that tree subtrees are nested or disjoint — the property fails with cycles.
4 · Lower bound Lemma + triangle inequality: any walk of length ℓ changes Σ|h_e| by at most ℓ, so Σ|δ_e| ≤ d(x, y).
5 · Upper bound Post-order algorithm: process edges leaves→root, moving |δ_e| tokens at each edge. Total moves = Σ|δ_e|, so d(x, y) ≤ Σ|δ_e|. The algorithm terminates at y (not some other config) by h-injectivity: on a tree, h(z) = h(y) implies z = y, proved by leaf-induction.
6 · QED Sandwich: Σ|δ_e| ≤ d(x,y) ≤ Σ|δ_e|, so d(x, y) = Σ|h_e(x) − h_e(y)|. ∎
